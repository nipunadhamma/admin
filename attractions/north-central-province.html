Journey Through Sri Lanka's Ancient Heart

North Central Province is one of Sri Lanka's greatest destinations for history, religion, archaeology and wildlife. The province is home to the ancient capitals of Anuradhapura and Polonnaruwa, magnificent stupas, sacred Buddhist sites, ancient monasteries, historic reservoirs and national parks where visitors can experience Sri Lanka's remarkable natural heritage.

🌳 Sacred Buddhist Heritage

Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi

One of the most sacred Buddhist sites in Sri Lanka, the Jaya Sri Maha Bodhi is a sacred Bodhi tree in the ancient city of Anuradhapura. It is traditionally regarded as a sapling brought from the original Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya in India by Sanghamitta Theri. The sacred tree has remained an important centre of Buddhist devotion and pilgrimage for centuries and is the foremost site of worship among the Atamasthana.

📍 Anuradhapura Sacred City, Anuradhapura District

🛕 Ancient Buddhist Heritage

Ruwanwelisaya Stupa

Ruwanwelisaya is one of the most revered stupas in Sri Lanka and one of the most important sacred places in Anuradhapura. Built during the reign of King Dutugemunu, the great white stupa has become an enduring symbol of Sri Lankan Buddhist architecture, devotion and ancient engineering. The monument continues to attract large numbers of pilgrims and visitors throughout the year.

📍 Anuradhapura Sacred City, Anuradhapura District
🗺️ View Map
🛕 Ancient Stupa

Thuparamaya

Thuparamaya is traditionally regarded as the first stupa built in Sri Lanka following the introduction of Buddhism to the island. It was constructed during the reign of King Devanampiyatissa and is believed to enshrine a sacred relic associated with the Buddha. The present stupa stands within the remains of an ancient monastic complex and is surrounded by historic stone pillars that reflect the architectural heritage of early Anuradhapura.

📍 Anuradhapura Sacred City, Anuradhapura District
🗺️ View Map
🏛️ Ancient Architecture

Lovamahapaya

Lovamahapaya, also known as the Brazen Palace, was once a magnificent multi-storey structure associated with the ancient Mahavihara monastery. According to historical accounts, the building was originally constructed during the reign of King Dutugemunu. Today, the remaining rows of stone pillars provide visitors with a glimpse into the scale and architectural ambition of ancient Anuradhapura.

📍 Between Sri Maha Bodhi and Ruwanwelisaya, Anuradhapura District
🗺️ View Map
🛕 Monastic Heritage

Abhayagiri Stupa

Abhayagiri is one of the great monastic complexes of ancient Anuradhapura and one of the eight sacred places of the city. The enormous stupa stands at the centre of an extensive archaeological landscape that includes ancient monasteries, meditation spaces, ponds, sculptures and other remains. The site offers a fascinating view of the religious, cultural and architectural development of ancient Sri Lanka.

📍 Abhayagiri Monastic Complex, Anuradhapura District
🗺️ View Map
🏛️ Ancient Buddhist Architecture

Jetavanaramaya Stupa

Jetavanaramaya is one of the most impressive ancient stupas in Anuradhapura and forms the centrepiece of the historic Jetavana monastic complex. Constructed during the reign of King Mahasena, the monument demonstrates the extraordinary engineering skills of ancient Sri Lankan builders. The surrounding archaeological remains provide further evidence of the importance of Jetavana as a major centre of Buddhist learning and monastic life.

📍 Jetavana Monastic Complex, Anuradhapura District
🗺️ View Map
🛕 Sacred Stupa

Mirisavetiya Stupa

Mirisavetiya is one of the eight sacred places of Anuradhapura and is traditionally associated with King Dutugemunu. According to historical tradition, the stupa was built after a sacred object connected with the king was left at the site and could not be moved. The peaceful surroundings and the historic monastery remains make Mirisavetiya a meaningful destination for pilgrims and visitors interested in the spiritual heritage of ancient Sri Lanka.

📍 Near Tissa Wewa, Anuradhapura District

🛕 Ancient Buddhist Heritage

Lankarama

Lankarama is one of the eight sacred places of Anuradhapura and is known for its peaceful setting and distinctive ancient architectural remains. The stupa is surrounded by stone pillars that are believed to be associated with an ancient circular shrine structure. Its quieter atmosphere provides visitors with an opportunity to experience another important part of the religious and archaeological heritage of the ancient capital.
